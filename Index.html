The Panic of 1907

An immersive chapter on the crisis that reshaped American banking.

What happened?

A financial panic unfolded in October 1907 after the failure of the Knickerbocker Trust Company. Confidence collapsed, and depositors rushed to withdraw funds.

Why did it happen?

Historians point to a fragile trust-company system, speculative financing, and a lack of centralized lender-of-last-resort capacity in the United States at that time.

Why did Morgan matter?

Morgan helped coordinate private liquidity, convene bankers, and organize support for threatened institutions. His role is documented in contemporary accounts.

What were the limitations?

Private intervention was ad hoc and depended on a small circle of financiers. It exposed structural weaknesses rather than solving them permanently.

How did it influence American banking?

The crisis is widely cited as a catalyst for monetary reform, culminating in the creation of the Federal Reserve System in 1913.

Modern JPMorgan Chase

A clear distinction between J. Pierpont Morgan and the modern firm that carries his name's legacy.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: J. Pierpont Morgan (1837–1913) and JPMorgan Chase & Co. are not the same entity. The modern firm traces part of its heritage to predecessor institutions associated with Morgan's era, but its current structure, operations, and governance are the product of a long, complex evolution.
